Quick Farm Tips

Raise the floor

A slatted floor 3–4 ft above ground lets droppings fall through. The shed stays dry and worm infection drops sharply.

Always-clean water

Use automatic float-valve drinkers, fix them at shoulder height, and scrub them weekly. Dirty water is a top cause of diarrhoea.

Grow your own fodder

Co-4 grass, subabul, agathi and moringa on even half an acre cut feed cost and give year-round green fodder.

Goat Farming Questions

How do I start goat farming with a small budget?

Start with about 10 does and 1 buck of a breed that suits your area. Build a raised, well-ventilated shed, arrange green fodder, and vaccinate and deworm before buying more. Expand only after 1–2 kidding cycles when you understand your costs.

Is goat farming profitable?

It can be, but profit depends on management. The main drivers are low kid mortality, more kids per doe, controlled feed cost, and selling at the right weight and season (Bakrid, Pongal, Deepavali). Keep records for every animal.

Are there loans or subsidies for goat farming?

Yes. Bank loans linked to NABARD and schemes under the National Livestock Mission, plus Tamil Nadu Animal Husbandry Department programmes, have offered subsidies. Eligibility and subsidy percentage change, so check the current scheme at your nearest veterinary dispensary or bank.

What should I feed my goats?

Most of the diet should be roughage: green fodder (Co-4 grass, subabul, agathi, moringa, hedge lucerne, cowpea) plus dry fodder. Add a concentrate mix in smaller amounts, more for pregnant, milking and growing animals, plus mineral mixture and a salt lick. Change feed gradually, never suddenly.

How often should I deworm goats?

Deworm based on a faecal egg count or your vet's advice, commonly every 3 months and more often in the rainy season. Dose by body weight, rotate drug classes as advised, and use a drenching gun for accurate dosing. Pale eyelids can signal heavy worm infection (anaemia).
